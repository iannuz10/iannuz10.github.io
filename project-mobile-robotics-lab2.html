Mobile Robotics: Robust Control for 2D Trajectory Tracking

Comparing Static Feedback and Lyapunov-based control laws for differential-drive and bicycle-kinematic robots under actuator saturation and model uncertainty.

At a Glance

  • Situation: Autonomous 2D robots, such as the differential drive (2-0) and bicycle (1-1) models, require precise trajectory tracking that can withstand real-world non-linearities like wheel velocity saturation and chassis calibration errors.
  • Task: Implement and evaluate Static Feedback and Lyapunov-based control strategies to determine which approach offers superior resilience against 30% calibration errors in wheel radius and chassis dimensions.
  • Action: I derived the kinematic mapping matrices (K) for both robot types and implemented non-linear control laws in MATLAB/Simulink. I tuned gains (e.g., $K_p$, $K_x$, $K_y$, $K_\theta$) to minimize an energy function $W$ representing posture error.
  • Result: Lyapunov-based control proved significantly more robust; it maintained circular motion even during wheel saturation and kept tracking error nearly negligible (reaching 0) during chassis calibration errors, whereas Static Feedback diverged.

Technical Deep Dive

Kinematic Models & Jacobians

The project focused on two distinct kinematic architectures:

  • Differential Drive (2-0): Controlled via linear velocity $v$ and angular velocity $\omega$.
  • Bicycle-like (1-1): Controlled via linear velocity $v$ and steering orientation rate $\dot{\beta}$.

For Static Feedback, I derived the decoupling matrix $K$ to link control inputs to the velocity of a tracking point $x_p$ located at a distance $d$ ahead of the robot: $$\dot{x}_{p}=K(\theta).u \implies K=\begin{bmatrix}\cos(\theta)&-d \sin(\theta)\\ \sin(\theta)&d \cos(\theta)\end{bmatrix}$$.

Lyapunov Stability Implementation

To ensure global stability, I implemented a candidate Lyapunov function $W$ to represent the state error: $$W=\frac{1}{2}(x_{e}^{2}+y_{e}^{2}+\frac{\theta_{e}^{2}}{K_{y}})$$.

I derived the control law such that the time derivative $\dot{W}$ became negative definite: $$\dot{W}=-K_{x}x_{e}^{2}-K_{y}v_{r}y_{e}^{2}\frac{\sin(\theta_{e})}{\theta_{e}}-\frac{K_{\theta}\theta_{e}^{2}}{K_{y}}$$. This ensures the robot's error converges to zero regardless of the initial displacement.

Robustness Analysis Findings

By injecting systematic errors into the Simulink model, I observed critical performance differences:

  • Calibration Errors: When the wheel radius was miscalibrated by 30%, the Static Feedback controller caused the robot to orbit inside the reference path. The Lyapunov controller, however, remained stable because it does not rely on perfect model inversion.
  • Actuator Saturation: Under wheel velocity limits, the differential robot exhibited understeering. However, the Lyapunov energy function still converged toward zero, suggesting the path was followed as closely as physical limits allowed.
  • Orientation Saturation: For the bicycle model, orientation limits forced the robot into a "minimal circular motion," tracking the frequency of the desired point even when it couldn't match the radius.
