Hybrid Localization: Extended Kalman Filter for Differential Robots

Implementing an EKF in MATLAB to fuse odometry with Reed sensor magnet detection, correcting for systematic errors and sensor noise in complex trajectories.

At a Glance

  • Situation: Differential robots accumulate odometry errors from wheel slippage, squishy wheels, and chassis rigidity issues. To maintain an accurate pose, the robot must detect external magnetic markers (magnets) to reset its uncertainty.
  • Task: Characterize the mobile robot's noise profiles, determine initial position uncertainty, and implement the Jacobian matrices for a functional Extended Kalman Filter (EKF).
  • Action: I derived the $A$, $B$, and $C$ matrices for the EKF linearization. I calculated measurement variances ($\sigma_x, \sigma_y$) by analyzing magnet detection patterns (e.g., 20mm worst-case range) and tuned the $\sigma$ parameter to separate valid readings from noise using Mahalanobis distance thresholds.
  • Result: The EKF successfully corrected trajectory drift in circular, diagonal, and loop tests. Using a tuned $\sigma = 0.1$ and a 95% confidence Mahalanobis threshold, the filter effectively dumped initial orientation errors and corrected for 3-second "blind spots" where only odometry was available.

Technical Deep Dive

Mathematical Framework

The core of the localization algorithm relies on linearizing the non-linear evolution and observation models. I implemented the following Jacobians to compute the Kalman Gain:

$$A=\begin{bmatrix}1&0&-v \cdot s\theta\\ 0&1&v \cdot c\theta\\ 0&0&1\end{bmatrix}, B=\begin{bmatrix}c\theta&0\\ s\theta&0\\ 0&1\end{bmatrix}$$

I also defined the observation matrix $C$, which relates small state displacements to the expected magnetic sensor measurements based on the robot's distance from detected magnets.

Characterizing Sensor Noise

A significant portion of the lab involved determining the variances for the measurement noise matrix ($Q_\gamma$):

  • $\sigma_x$: Based on a maximum detection range of 20mm in the worst case, I assumed a uniform distribution: $\sigma_x = 20/\sqrt{12} \text{ mm}$.
  • $\sigma_y$: Since a magnet can trigger up to two adjacent Reed sensors across a 10mm span, I used $\sigma_y = 10/\sqrt{12} \text{ mm}$.
  • Mahalanobis Distance: To distinguish between real magnets and sensor noise, I set a threshold of 2.4477 (calculated via `chi2inv` for 2 DOF at 95% confidence).

Trajectory Analysis & Insights

Testing the implementation across various datasets provided critical insights into the EKF's robustness:

  • Correction Gaps: In the twoloops.txt test, I identified a 3-second gap without magnet detection. The EKF showed an increasing posture variance during this period, which was immediately reduced ("corrected") upon hitting the next magnet at timestamp 17s.
  • Initial Error Recovery: I demonstrated that the EKF could dump significant initial human "pointing" errors. Even when starting at incorrect coordinates (e.g., $(0,0)$ instead of $(0,27)$), the EKF made a sharp correction at the first magnet encounter to realign with the true path.
  • Drift Attribution: While the odometry path showed a perfect circle (ignoring physics), the EKF revealed slight drift caused by non-point wheel contact and slippage, providing a more "honest" representation of the robot's real-world path.
