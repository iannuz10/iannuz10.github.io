Robotics: Modelling & Operational Control of Serial Manipulators

Implementing Geometric and Kinematic models in C++ to achieve precise Cartesian space control for Turret, KR16, and UR10 industrial robots.

At a Glance

  • Situation: Accurate manipulation requires three fundamental models: Direct Geometric (DGM), Inverse Geometric (IGM), and Direct Kinematic. These allow a robot to translate joint angles into end-effector poses and vice-versa.
  • Task: Build these models for three distinct serial robots (Turret, KUKA KR16, and UR10) and implement high-level control strategies like Point-to-Point and Jacobian-based operational control.
  • Action: I established reference frames using Denavit-Hartenberg (DH) conventions and solved the IGM using trigonometric solvers (Types 1-8). I implemented C++ controllers to map desired operational velocities to joint velocities via the Jacobian pseudo-inverse ($J^+$).
  • Result: Successfully demonstrated linear trajectory tracking in Cartesian space. By interpolating poses rather than just joint angles, I eliminated the "curved" traces typical of simple joint-space movements, achieving precise end-effector manipulation.

Technical Deep Dive

Geometric Modelling (DH Parameters)

The foundation of the project was the systematic placement of reference frames following the Khalil-Kleinfinger rules. For each robot, I compiled Denavit-Hartenberg tables to define the transformation from the base link to the end-effector.

For the KR16, the presence of a spherical wrist allowed for kinematic decoupling. I focused first on the translation generated by the first three joints before solving the rotation defined by the wrist.

Solving the Inverse Geometric Model (IGM)

The IGM is critical for operational control. I utilized a library of custom trigonometric solvers to handle various joint configurations:

  • Revolute & Prismatic: Solved for $q_1, q_2, q_3$ configurations using Type 2 ($Xs_i + Yc_i = Z$) and Type 7 solvers.
  • Singularity Handling: Implemented logic to handle cases where $s_5 = 0$, where I arbitrarily set $q_4$ to its current value to maintain stability.

Operational Control & Jacobian

To achieve smooth movement along a direct 3D line, I implemented Jacobian Operational Control. This maps desired Cartesian velocities ($v, \omega$) to joint velocities ($\dot{q}$): $$\dot{q} = J^{+} \begin{bmatrix} v \\ \omega \end{bmatrix}$$

I used a degree 3 polynomial interpolation function to ensure joint velocity and acceleration constraints ($v_{Max}, a_{Max}$) were never exceeded, resulting in seamless pose transitions.

Comparative Results

The lab highlighted the difference between Point-to-Point Control and Operational Control:

  • Point-to-Point: Results in a curved trace as each joint moves independently toward its final value.
  • Operational Control (IGM/Jacobian): Results in a straight-line trace by continuously interpolating intermediary poses in Cartesian space.
